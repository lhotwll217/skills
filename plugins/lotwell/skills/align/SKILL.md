# Align

An **agreement** is the shared understanding work proceeds from: it preserves the requester's intent, makes the assumptions the work depends on explicit, and leaves methods to whoever owns execution.

## Establish the agreement

Scale the exchange with the ask's **vagueness**, how **load-bearing** each assumption is, and the **consequences of being wrong**: a settled request gets a one-sentence say-back. An assumption is load-bearing when the result depends on it.

1. **Recover what is settled.** Read the original request, relevant sources, and subsequent decisions or corrections. Resolve factual questions from available evidence before asking the requester. Done when every load-bearing assumption is settled by evidence or a decision, or marked open.

2. **Say it back.** Present to the requester, in their meaning:
   - **Purpose:** what the work is for, and why.
   - **Result:** what the work produces, or the questions it explores.
   - **Assumptions:** each load-bearing one, with the evidence or decision that settles it, or marked open. For each open one, say whether the agreed work can explore it or it needs a decision first; ask for those decisions together, with a recommendation.
   - **Authority:** what you decide yourself, and what returns to the requester or others.
   - **Done when:** the evidence that makes the result usable or supports the next decision.
   - **Shape:** the smallest view from the `show-me` skill of any parts, flow, or before and after in the work.


3. **Get confirmation.** End every say-back, however settled the work looks, by asking the requester to confirm or correct it, and start the work once they confirm. A confirmed shared purpose with explicitly open questions is enough to start joint exploration.

## Authority

The agreement carries the initiating request's authority. A request to build or act authorizes the aligned work. A request to explore or discuss authorizes that exploration or discussion; changes to files or external state need authority in the request or existing agreement. Interpret new questions and remarks in context, preserving the work already authorized.

Take decisions beyond your delegated authority to the relevant decision maker: choices retained by the requester return to them; changes affecting other contributors go to the person coordinating them. Work that remains useful under the current agreement can continue.

## Changes to the agreement

When a correction, consequential reframe, or change of responsibility alters the agreement, say back **what changed and what still holds** and confirm it as in step 3. Carry forward every unaffected requirement. Local method changes within an unchanged agreement remain yours; make consequential effects visible to affected participants.

## Handoffs and durable context

At a handoff, the receiver runs steps 1 to 3 with the sender as its requester. The sender compares that say-back with the original request and the confirmed agreement, since a restatement of the sender's brief cannot show fidelity, corrects differences that would change the work, and confirms once none remain. Differences that change the agreement itself go back to its requester.

Keep a small request's agreement in the conversation. When work spans sessions, is delegated, or carries consequential decisions, record the current agreement in its existing issue, spec, or project document. The record is complete when it carries every say-back item and the sources behind each, and the handoff links it.
