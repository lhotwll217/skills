---
name: ask-the-right-questions
description: "Find the few questions that matter most about a problem before anyone answers it."
disable-model-invocation: true
---

Find the few questions about the problem in front of you whose answers would most change what we believe the work is for and what we would do. Treat the problem as **ill-structured**: its goal and limits are part of what the questions must find.

**Locate the difficulty at the level of purpose.** State what the system or work is for, what it needs to do that, and whether it has it. The most important question is usually whether it has what it needs. Read complaints, symptoms, and earlier fixes as evidence about that need. Done when each need is named with the evidence that it is met, unmet, or unknown.

**Widen the readings.** Write materially different readings of what is wanted and what stands in the way, including readings from comparable systems and from the people affected, and keep your first reading among them. Done when every reading has each of its **presuppositions** written down.

**Check every presupposition.** Check each candidate question's presuppositions against the evidence. A question resting on a presupposition the evidence does not establish is **loaded**: ask about the presupposition instead, since that is the earlier question. Done when every loaded question is replaced by a question about its presupposition.

**Every question you present is:**
- **Purpose-level:** it asks about the purpose, a need, or whether a need is met.
- **Unloaded:** whatever it takes for granted, including that one of its options holds, is established by the evidence.
- **About the need:** where a remedy or a symptom prompted it, it asks about the need behind them.
- **Inquiry:** it is put to the problem; a question that grills the owner for a preference or detail sits below this bar.
- **Bare:** one line for the question, one line for what its answer would change.

**Rank and cut.** Weigh how vague the point is, how load-bearing the answer is, and the cost of being wrong, plus any other factor the case presents. Present the questions alone: the few at the top as a numbered list, most important first, with no preamble or closing. Done when each presented question meets every clause above and outranks every question left off.
