# Finding research documents

Find the work itself and record which edition or version was obtained.

## Identify and locate

Recover title, authors, year, and DOI or book edition from the existing research. Inspect local files before downloading again. Classify each as full text, extract, source note, or metadata.

Resolve the DOI or publisher record to establish identity. Search author and institutional repositories, journal archives, and scholarly archives by exact title and author. For older works, check digitized editions and native HTML or EPUB transcriptions. Prefer the requested edition; record differences when only another edition is available.

For reflowable reading, prefer a verified native EPUB or HTML over a scan. Retain a paginated original for citation and checking formulas, tables, and OCR. An anthology may contain the original paper; identify its actual pages before calling it acquired.

## Verify and record

Verify the downloaded format by file signature and parser, then inspect the title or first page, the middle, and the ending. Compare complete original pages, not crops derived from an OCR text layer: unrecognized regions can be absent from both extracted text and its bounding boxes. Compare the table of contents with chapter or section transitions throughout the body. Check page continuity and text length against archive metadata where available; a first page and intact ending can conceal omitted middle chapters. HTTP success can deliver a bot challenge or login page. Match authors, title, date, and version to the requested citation; confirm that the whole work is present.

Keep a manifest with one record per requested work. Include full citation, DOI or canonical link, actual retrieval URL, access date, local path, format, edition, page count when known, and separate acquisition, completeness, and validation statuses. Record failed acquisition routes and the next legitimate access route separately. Save downloaded originals under descriptive names and link existing originals rather than duplicate them.

A source note, abstract, search snippet, or incomplete preview stays labelled as such. A fetched transcription keeps its retrieval provenance and OCR limitations. For a borrowed or purchased source, record the ordinary access route and leave acquisition pending when credentials or payment are required.

## Hand back

Publish the manifest early so ready texts can proceed independently. Provide a download checklist that distinguishes acquired full texts from remaining access needs, links each citation to its source, and points to local originals. State which files support immediate reading and which need extraction or OCR before conversion. Downstream excerpts preserve printed pages, PDF page offsets, or web section locators according to the source.
